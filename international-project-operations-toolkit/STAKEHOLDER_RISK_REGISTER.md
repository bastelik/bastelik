### Engagement prompts

- What does this stakeholder need from the project?
- What decisions or resources can they influence?
- What concerns, incentives or constraints may affect participation?
- What information do they need, in what language and at what frequency?
- Is there a risk of excluding affected groups from consultation or decisions?

Use influence and interest as planning aids, not as substitutes for meaningful participation. Consider accessibility, language, power differences and safeguarding.

## Risk register

A risk is an uncertain event or condition that may affect objectives. An issue is something that has already happened and requires action.

| ID | Risk statement (cause → event → effect) | Likelihood | Impact | Rating | Owner | Mitigation / contingency | Trigger / review date |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R-01 | | 1–5 | 1–5 | L × I | | | |

### Suggested rating guide

**Likelihood**
1. Rare
2. Unlikely
3. Possible
4. Likely
5. Almost certain

**Impact**
1. Negligible
2. Minor
3. Moderate
4. Major
5. Severe

A simple score is likelihood × impact. Define thresholds with the sponsor; do not assume that the same numeric score has the same meaning across projects. A low-frequency safety, legal or reputational risk may still require immediate escalation.

### Risk statement structure

Write each risk as:

> Because of [cause], [uncertain event] may occur, resulting in [effect on an objective].

Avoid vague entries such as “communication risk.” Specify the failure mode and consequence.

### Response options

- **Avoid:** change the plan so the threat cannot occur.
- **Reduce:** lower likelihood or impact.
- **Transfer / share:** allocate part of the exposure through an agreement or partner, while retaining oversight.
- **Accept:** consciously retain the risk, with monitoring and contingency where appropriate.
- **Exploit / enhance:** for opportunities, increase the likelihood or benefit.

## Review routine

At each review:
1. Reassess likelihood, impact and proximity.
2. Confirm each item has an accountable owner.
3. Check whether mitigations are actually completed and effective.
4. Identify new triggers, assumptions or external changes.
5. Escalate items beyond the team's authority.
6. Close only when the exposure has ended or is formally accepted.
7. Record decisions and communicate changes to affected stakeholders.
